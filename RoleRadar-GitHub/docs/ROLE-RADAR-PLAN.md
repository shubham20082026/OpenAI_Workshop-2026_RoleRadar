# RoleRadar product scope

**Pitch:** Find real remote opportunities from your skills, then inspect the evidence behind the fit.

Target group: students looking for internships and early-career roles. The student has reported scarce opportunities; no measured cause or improvement is assumed.

## MVP and cut list

Built: real source retrieval; transparent mention matching; country/type filters; hypothetical skill reranking; source-state checks; accounts; private profile, shortlist, notes, history, AI exchanges; export and deletion; explicit guest/demo operation.

Cut: automated applications, email outreach, CV uploads, employer verification, a trained recommender, eligibility certification, guaranteed interview scores, all-job-board coverage, onsite campus placement feeds, background alerts and payments.

The AI component is optional interpretation through a manual ChatGPT handoff. The source retrieval, ranking and quote validation are implemented product logic. A normal chat can explain a supplied job; it does not automatically reproduce this account workspace and source-status workflow. Do not claim that no chatbot could ever implement similar tooling.

## Competition filter

Likely neighboring entries: AI internship recommender, résumé/job matcher, career chatbot. This remains a crowded category. Source evidence, truthful freshness/status handling and skill counterfactuals improve the demo, but no 8/10 distinctiveness or winning claim has been established.

Opening demonstration: search with Python/Git, preview SQL as a hypothetical skill, show the actual number of retrieved roles whose mention count changes, then open one exact source quote and check source status. Read the displayed numbers; do not invent or rehearse a fixed count that may change.

## Three-user validation — still to do

Ask three reachable students to find three roles they would consider applying for using their current method, then using RoleRadar on comparable tasks. Record completion time, roles they judged ineligible, misleading results, and whether the source evidence changed a decision. Vary task order where practical. Collect agreement; do not collect CVs, passwords or private employer messages. A tiny convenience sample does not establish general hiring impact.

Honest story format: “Students reported difficulty finding opportunities. I observed [specific behavior during testing]. I built a source-backed search workspace. In [actual small test], [measured change].” The bracketed facts are research to perform, not claims to copy into a submission.

## Next build priorities after evidence

1. Fix the most common observed search failure.
2. Add a second permitted source only if coverage is the measured bottleneck.
3. Complete public mail/redirect and HTTPS hosting setup.
4. Automate AI only if a free, reliable model path is available and genuinely improves task completion.
